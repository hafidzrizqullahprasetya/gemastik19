# The Impact of Job Vacancy Fraud and Its Prevention
**Authors:** Efendi*, Luthvi Febryka Nola**  
**Affiliation:** Center for Parliamentary Analysis, Expert Agency of the Indonesian House of Representatives (DPR RI), Jakarta, Indonesia  
**Publication:** Info Singkat Komisi IX, Vol. XVII, No. 24/II/PUSAKA/December 2025  

## Abstract
Job vacancy fraud has become increasingly rampant in recent years, emerging as a severe form of crime that inflicts significant harm on the public. The continuously evolving modus operandi—ranging from unofficial job offers to the malicious misuse of corporate identities—has trapped numerous job seekers, resulting in substantial losses. This phenomenon extends its detrimental effects beyond direct victims, causing broad consequences for job search platforms, legitimate companies, and even national security. This paper examines the multifaceted impacts of job vacancy fraud with the objective of formulating more comprehensive prevention strategies. The discussion reveals that the proliferation of such fraud causes both material and immaterial losses for job seekers, tarnishes the reputation of recruitment platforms and companies, and disrupts national security. Consequently, prevention efforts must be executed through regulatory strengthening, infrastructure improvement, enhanced digital and legal literacy, and the establishment of solid inter-agency coordination. In this context, Commission IX of the Indonesian House of Representatives (DPR RI) can play a pivotal role through its budgetary function to support infrastructure development, its legislative function to enforce stricter job vacancy reporting regulations within the Employment Bill, and its supervisory function to urge the government to elevate public literacy and fortify cross-institutional coordination.

## 1. Introduction
Over the past two years, the reduction in the unemployment rate has been marginal. In fact, from February 2024 to February 2025, the number of unemployed individuals increased from 7.19 million to 7.28 million (BPS, 2025). Suppressing unemployment remains exceedingly difficult due to the limited availability of job opportunities, a consequence of economic slowdowns, the continuous growth of the working-age population, and the rapid pace of digitalization (Larasati, 2025). This challenging economic landscape is further exacerbated by rising living costs.

Under these pressures, job seekers become highly vulnerable to seemingly promising job offers that are, in reality, sophisticated scams. The Cybersecurity Research Center Indonesia reports that 4 out of 10 job seekers on online platforms have received suspicious job offers within the last 12 months, with approximately 12% of them falling victim to actual fraud (Cityguard, 2025). Furthermore, SEEK, the parent company of Jobstreet and JobsDB, has identified Indonesia as a primary hotspot for job vacancy fraud, accounting for 38% of all fraud attempts in the Asia-Pacific region and 62% of the total cases in Asia ("Indonesia Jadi Pusat Penipuan Kerja", 2025).

The proliferation of job vacancy fraud in Indonesia has positioned the country as a central hub for such criminal activities in the Asia-Pacific. In 2024 alone, over a thousand Indonesian citizens became victims of human trafficking (Tindak Pidana Perdagangan Orang or TPPO) that originated from fake job offers. This paper explores the various impacts of job vacancy fraud to help formulate more comprehensive and effective prevention measures.

## 2. The Impact of Job Vacancy Fraud
Job vacancy fraud is a serious criminal act that subjects job seekers to various forms of loss, and in extreme cases, leads to human trafficking. In 2024, more than a thousand Indonesian citizens fell victim to TPPO linked to fraudulent job offers (Nadira, 2025). The extensive impacts of this fraud necessitate immediate and multifaceted interventions.

### 2.1. Impact on Victims (Job Seekers)
The most direct impact is borne by the job seekers themselves, who suffer both material and immaterial losses. 
*   **Material Losses:** The most common modus operandi involves scammers demanding upfront payments, often disguised as "guarantee money" or administrative fees. The Directorate of Cyber Crime, Bareskrim Polri, recorded that out of 823 victims of fraud between 2022 and 2024, the total financial loss suffered by job seekers reached IDR 59 billion (Nadira, 2025). 
*   **Data Misuse and Identity Theft:** Material losses also stem from the misuse of personal data, such as bank account numbers, National Identity Cards (KTP), and other sensitive information. For instance, dozens of residents in East Jakarta had their KTPs exploited by fraudsters to apply for illegal online loans, trapping the victims in debt and subjecting them to severe psychological pressure from debt collectors (Nurhadi, 2024).
*   **Immaterial and Psychological Losses:** The psychological toll is a significant immaterial loss. Victims frequently experience a loss of self-confidence, anxiety, and a marked reduction in social participation (Cityguard.co.id, 2025).

### 2.2. Impact on Job Search Platforms
The rampant occurrence of job fraud severely impacts online recruitment platforms such as Jobstreet, Indeed, and Glints by eroding public trust. This decline in credibility makes job seekers hesitant to utilize these platforms, thereby slowing down the national recruitment process. If left unaddressed, this reluctance can disrupt the entire national employment ecosystem, as the public loses faith in digital job-search channels. Consequently, platforms are forced to continuously upgrade their security protocols. For example, between July 2024 and June 2025, Jobstreet rejected 3,600 corporate recruiters who failed verification, closed 650 high-risk accounts, and deleted 2,800 fraudulent job postings following investigations (Nadira, 2025).

### 2.3. Impact on Legitimate Companies
When fraudsters misuse a company's name or identity, the legitimate company's reputation and public image are directly tarnished. Prospective employees become overly cautious or actively avoid recruitment processes associated with the impersonated company. This loss of trust not only damages the company's brand but also hinders its ability to attract potential talent. A notable case involved Basco City Mall, which had to issue public clarifications and cooperate with law enforcement after 138 job seekers were defrauded by scammers using the mall's name (Iriawan, 2025).

### 2.4. Impact on National Security
On a macro level, job vacancy fraud has evolved into a gateway for organized crime, including human trafficking (TPPO) that targets thousands of Indonesian citizens. This transformation indicates that job fraud is no longer merely an individual or consumer protection issue; it has escalated into a severe threat to national and regional security.

## 3. Prevention Efforts
Given the extensive and severe impacts of job vacancy fraud, immediate and comprehensive countermeasures are imperative. The following efforts are proposed:

1.  **Strengthening Regulatory Frameworks:** Currently, Article 36 paragraph (2) of Law No. 13 of 2003 on Manpower does not mandate companies to report their job vacancies. This obligation was only recently introduced in Presidential Regulation No. 57 of 2023, which imposes merely administrative sanctions, such as written warnings, for non-compliance. Job vacancy reporting should be explicitly mandated within the core Manpower Law, accompanied by strict sanctions, including the revocation of business licenses.
2.  **Strengthening Infrastructure and Law Enforcement Capacity:** It is crucial to enhance digital forensic facilities, develop advanced cyber-tracking technologies, and provide specialized training for Law Enforcement Agencies (APH) across all regions. These steps are vital for improving the identification and investigation of cyber-enabled job fraud.
3.  **Increasing Digital and Legal Literacy:** Public education must be prioritized, especially for vulnerable demographics such as fresh graduates, informal workers, and young internet users. Educational programs should cover digital rights, guides on recognizing fraud modus operandi, and clear reporting mechanisms for crimes involving data misuse or fake fee demands. Preventive campaigns via social media, online educational platforms, and collaborations with local communities and educational institutions can significantly raise awareness and mitigate digital crimes.
4.  **Enhancing Inter-Agency Coordination:** Accelerating law enforcement and victim protection requires robust coordination among the Ministry of Manpower (Kemnaker), the Ministry of Communication and Digital (Komdigi), and the National Police. To this end, Kemnaker has established a Task Force (Satgas) for Handling Job Vacancy Hoaxes, involving the National Cyber and Crypto Agency (BSSN), Komdigi, the Police, and regional Manpower Offices. Kemnaker has also launched a Job Vacancy Hoax Prevention Command Post, accessible via call centers, WhatsApp, websites, and official social media channels.

## 4. The Role of Commission IX of the Indonesian House of Representatives
In the context of the aforementioned prevention efforts, Commission IX of the DPR RI (which oversees health, manpower, and social security) holds a critical position:
*   **Legislative Function:** The ongoing discussion of the Employment Bill (RUU Ketenagakerjaan) presents a crucial momentum to legally mandate job vacancy reporting and enforce stringent sanctions for violators.
*   **Budgetary Function:** Commission IX must advocate for budget allocations to support the development of digital monitoring systems and the capacity building of manpower inspectors.
*   **Supervisory Function:** Through its oversight capabilities, Commission IX can urge the government to aggressively promote public digital literacy. Furthermore, it can push for the formalization of the cross-ministerial Task Force via a Presidential Regulation, ensuring that all involved agencies share an equal level of commitment and seriousness in executing their duties.

---

## 5. Conclusion
Job vacancy fraud inflicts widespread and devastating impacts on society. For job seekers, it results in severe material and immaterial losses. For recruitment platforms and legitimate companies, it tarnishes reputations and diminishes public trust. More broadly, the proliferation of these scams threatens national security and regional stability. Therefore, prevention must be executed through a combination of regulatory strengthening, infrastructure enhancement, digital and legal literacy programs, and reinforced inter-agency coordination. Commission IX of the DPR RI can actively drive these initiatives by leveraging its budgetary, legislative, and supervisory functions to safeguard the national employment ecosystem.
